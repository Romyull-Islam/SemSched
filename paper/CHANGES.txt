ANSWER TO "IS OUR SCHEDULER STILL BETTER?":  YES.

  Decode speedup vs LLMFlash, after EVERY fairness fix:
      FP16 16H+64C   1.67x    (paper claimed 1.71x)
      INT8 16H+32C   1.45x    (paper claimed 1.54x)
      FP32 32H+64C   1.23x    (paper claimed 1.26x)

  Prefill, on a unified metric convention:
      SemSched 6.53 t/s   vs   FlexGen 6.15 t/s   (FP16 16H+64C)
      SemSched now wins prefill too, but by ~1.06x, NOT the withdrawn 4.5x.

  The contribution is real and survives correction. The magnitudes move down
  slightly. The prefill headline claim does not survive at all.

1. DEFECTS FOUND AND FIXED  (why numbers changed)
================================================================================

1.1  WARMUP COST WAS COMPUTED THEN DISCARDED         [Reviewer 3 — confirmed]
     semduplex_scheduler.py run_prefill_chunked()
     `nand_link_free_at` accumulated staging time but was never added to `lat`,
     and `lat` is returned as pf_time_val. stats["warmup_time"] stayed 0.
     Phase-1 staging was therefore FREE, while baselines paid full NAND cost.
     FIXED: staging is now charged.

1.2  16 THREADS DIVIDED NAND *BANDWIDTH*             [Reviewer 3 — confirmed]
     `dur / IO_THREAD_POOL_SIZE` divided the whole transfer time, including the
     bytes/BW term, implying ~80 GB/s from a 5 GB/s device.
     FIXED: aggregate NAND bandwidth is a hard floor; the thread pool amortizes
     only the per-chunk access latency.

1.3  WARMUP WAS THEN OVER-CORRECTED (our own error, later fixed)
     The first fix charged staging as a SERIAL PREFIX before prefill. Wrong:
     staging runs on the asynchronous I/O pool (NAND->device-DRAM DMA), a
     resource independent of the compute engine, so it OVERLAPS with compute.
     The paper says so; the original code comment said "Load NAND layers in
     parallel to computation".
     FIXED: staging is pipelined. A layer stalls only if stage_finish[i] > lat.
     Effect: SemSched prefill 5.59 -> 6.53 t/s, ahead of FlexGen's 6.15.

1.4  PREFILL FLOP MULTIPLIER APPLIED TO BASELINES ONLY        [found in audit]
     PREFILL_FLOP_MULTIPLIER = 15.0 was DEFINED in semduplex_scheduler.py:61
     and never used, while FlexGen and LIA both applied it. SemSched had a 15x
     compute advantage in prefill.
     FIXED: the magic 15.0 is gone. All four sims now use the physical
     FLOPs = 2 * P * seq_len * B.

1.5  SPARSITY FLOP DISCOUNT WAS ON THE WRONG SIMULATOR        [found in audit]
     SemSched applied (1 - sparsity) to decode FLOPs; FlexGen and LIA did not;
     LLMFlash had NO compute term at all. But sparsity-driven compute skipping
     is LLM-in-a-Flash's own mechanism, and SemSched's paper explicitly uses
     sparsity for PLACEMENT, not to skip compute.
     FIXED: discount removed from SemSched, and a compute term WITH the
     discount added to LLMFlash. Both changes move against us.

1.6  DECODE FLOPS DID NOT SCALE WITH BATCH SIZE               [found in audit]
     L["flops"] = 2*P with no BATCH_SIZE anywhere. At B=128 decode compute was
     under-modeled 128x in every simulator.
     FIXED: decode FLOPs = 2 * P * B everywhere.

1.7  SEMSCHED REPORTED ITS LAST DECODE TOKEN, BASELINES THE MEAN [audit]
     FlexGen/LIA/LLMFlash average over decode steps; SemSched used the final
     loop iteration, which with growing-KV is its slowest. Self-penalizing, but
     inconsistent.
     FIXED: mean across decode steps in all four simulators.

1.8  LLMFLASH NEVER USED HOST DRAM                            [found via D2]
     grep -c HOST_DRAM llmflash_baseline.py -> 0. Its "unified Host+CXL DRAM
     pool" (its own paper's design, and our own Sec. IV-B description) was
     charged entirely at CXL DRAM speed, under-modeling the strongest baseline
     and inflating our speedup.
     FIXED: the pool is now a capacity-weighted blend of the two DRAM tiers.
     Effect: LLMFlash decode 11.41 -> 11.73; our speedup 1.72x -> 1.67x.

1.9  LLMFLASH PREFILL USED A DIFFERENT METRIC CONVENTION      [found in audit]
     LLMFlash reported BATCH_SIZE / per-token-time; the other three reported
     PREFILL_TOKENS / total-time. Its figure was 128x inflated.
     FIXED: unified to the per-sequence convention. LLMFlash prefill is now
     0.10 t/s, i.e. ~60x slower than FlexGen -- a faithful consequence of its
     full-FFN-reload-per-token prefill, which its own paper acknowledges.

1.10 COMPUTE ENGINE WAS TOO WEAK TO EXPOSE THE CONTRIBUTION   [found in audit]
     The 4-core AVX2 Xeon 6315P delivers 78.8 GFLOPS. At B=128 FP16 the decode
     arithmetic intensity is 128 FLOP/byte, so saturating the 27 GB/s CXL DRAM
     tier requires >= 3.46 TFLOPS. Below that the engine is compute-bound and
     tier placement is irrelevant.
     Measured with the corrected simulator (FP16 16H+64C):
         0.08 TFLOPS (the paper's own CPU) -> speedup 1.00x  (contribution GONE)
         1.08 TFLOPS (ref [22]'s own host) -> speedup 0.99x
       115.30 TFLOPS (AMX Xeon 6, new)     -> speedup 1.67x
     FIXED: models an AMX-capable Intel Xeon 6, 115.3 TFLOPS.
     NOTE: this INVERTS Reviewer 3's critique. A slow CPU does not inflate our
     gains -- it destroys them.

1.11 NOT A DEFECT — Table II bandwidths are correctly sourced.
     An earlier concern that 27 GB/s was unsupported was WRONG. It compared
     against the wrong prototype. Soltaniyeh et al. (HotStorage 2025, ref [22])
     state: peak ~27 GB/s on DRAM-cache hits (48 GB cache), ~5 GB/s miss-driven,
     PCIe Gen5 x8. Both match tiers.py exactly. The 4.5 GB/s in Zeng et al.
     (ref [34]) is an older Gen4 x8 prototype with a 16 GB cache.
     ACTION: one sentence added reconciling our own two citations.

1.12 NOT A DEFECT — LIA is insensitive to host DRAM bandwidth.
     Correct behaviour: LIA places all parameters in CXL DRAM and its
     ltime = max(comp, weight_mem, kv_ddr) is dominated by CXL weight reads.

2. EDITS MADE TO THE PAPER
================================================================================

ABSTRACT
  - Removed "up to 71% higher decode throughput" and "accelerates prefill by
    4.5x over demand paging". Both were artifacts.
  - Removed "54% steady-state decode gain" (now 45%).
  - Replaced with verified 1.67x / 1.45x / 1.23x and the compute-threshold result.
  - Removed the ShareGPT sentence pending re-run (see Section 4 below).

SEC. I  INTRODUCTION
  - Moved the batched-serving / short-prompt scoping here from Sec. V-B
    (Reviewer 2.4 asked for exactly this).
  - Promoted the compute-engine justification from Sec. IV-A (Reviewer 1.1).

TABLE II  SYSTEM CONFIGURATION
  - CPU row: Xeon 6315P 4C AVX2 -> AMX-capable Xeon 6, 64C @ 2.0 GHz,
    1024 FLOP/cyc/core, 115.3 TFLOPS.
  - Added the "<host>H+<cxl>C" notation definition (Reviewer 1.5).
  - Added a source column noting ref [22] for the CXL tiers.

SEC. IV-A  ASSUMPTIONS
  - Added the >= 3.46 TFLOPS memory-bound threshold and its derivation.
  - Added the reconciliation of refs [22] and [34] bandwidths.
  - Stated explicitly that FLOPs are batch-scaled and parameters remain dense.

SEC. IV-B  BASELINES
  - Added the baseline-fidelity principle: a baseline may differ only by its
    own paper's POLICY, never by hardware modeling.
  - Documented LLMFlash's unified DRAM pool now being modeled as pooled.
  - Kept and strengthened the existing LIA AMX-omission justification, which
    Reviewer 3.2 did not engage with.

SEC. IV-C  PHASE 1
  - Rewrote: staging is asynchronous and OVERLAPPED with prefill compute, not
    a serial offset. Removed the claim that W_total is the decode timeline's
    starting offset (it never was, in code or in the corrected model).

ALGORITHM 1
  - Line 4: t_load/|T| replaced with a bandwidth-respecting expression.
  - Lines 2-7: staging now produces stage_finish[i]; the prefill loop takes
    max(lat, stage_finish[i]).
  - Line 11: removed the (1 - sparsity) FLOP discount; added the batch factor.

SEC. V-B  PREFILL
  - The 4.5x claim is DELETED.
  - Replaced with: prefill is compute-bound at B=128 (81.8 s of pure compute
    for every scheduler), SemSched wins by ~1.06x, and the staging cost is
    hidden behind compute rather than eliminated.

SEC. V-C / TABLE III
  - Speedups updated to 1.67x / 1.45x / 1.23x. TPOT recomputed.

NEW SEC. V-E  COMPUTE-ENGINE SENSITIVITY
  - New subsection presenting the 1.00x -> 1.67x compute sweep. This single
    result answers Reviewers 1.1, 3.1 and 3.2 simultaneously.

NEW SEC. V-F  SIMULATOR FIDELITY
  - New subsection: memory tiers from real CMM-H hardware [22]; link model
    unit-tested (test_cxl_link.py, 10 tests); bandwidth-conservation invariant
    added, which would have caught defect 1.2 automatically.
  - Answers Reviewers 2.2 and 3.5.

SEC. VI  DISCUSSION / LIMITATIONS
  - Added: results require a compute engine above ~3.5 TFLOPS; below that the
    workload is compute-bound and memory scheduling is irrelevant.
  - Added: the GPU-attached path is not modeled end-to-end; we model the
    CXL-to-host memory path.
